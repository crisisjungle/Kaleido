# Scenario Design

## Current Capability

The Step 2 scenario design workspace is a three-state flow: parameter configuration, generation in progress, and generated-result review. These states are mutually exclusive in the UI.

Users first confirm simulation parameters. The confirmation action remains visible outside the scrollable form. During generation, the form and incomplete output are replaced by progress guidance. After generation, the parameter form stays locked behind a dedicated input-snapshot entrance and the generated display tabs become available: risk objects, region division, Agent configuration, and relationship skeleton.

## Key Rules

- Step 2 consumes `semantic-input.v1` and writes `scenario_planning.v2`. Controlled event keys and policy actions are authoritative; local keyword matching is retained only for legacy or no-model compatibility input.
- School closure, workplace shutdown, transport restriction, shelter, evacuation, monitoring, resource dispatch, information release, repair, and compensation remain separate policy primitives. Their non-mergeable authority boundaries produce distinct RoleDemand records.
- Editable cards preserve all target IDs, expected effects, time, intensity, direction, source, and controlled semantic keys when they are serialized back to `/prepare`.
- Step 2 follows the result-first output contract in `docs/modules/result-first-output.md`. Preparation diagnostics may record success, failure, fallback, confidence, and unresolved references internally, but the formal review surface presents the validated risk, region, Agent, and relationship results directly. It does not add “generation succeeded”, “Agent creation succeeded”, or similar self-reporting UI.
- Analysis intensity is chosen only in Step 1 through the display levels `Light / Medium / High / Extra High / Ultra`; the stored machine values remain `light / medium / high / extra_high / ultra`, and the default display level is `High`. The compact `分析强度` control sits immediately left of the bottom-right `生成背景` action and must not render translated level names. The first accepted background-generation request locks the returned snapshot. Retries, regeneration, navigation back to Step 1, and Step 2 cannot change it.
- The durable Effort reference contains `effort_snapshot_id`, `effort_level`, `profile_version`, and `content_hash`. Step 2 displays it as a read-only locked value and must reject a missing or conflicting snapshot rather than reconstructing the choice from a route query.
- Step 2 no longer asks users to choose a hazard template, quick/deep search, mechanism sample count, Agent target count, start round, duration, or numeric intensity. New preparation requests always use the mechanism-planning architecture; `search_mode=deep_search` is retained only as an internal compatibility field and does not reopen a user choice.
- The ordinary input surface separates disaster events from policy measures. Event inputs expose only name, description, order, and target regions/facilities. Policy inputs expose only name, intent, and target regions/facilities. Internal hazard families, policy classes, mechanisms, timing, intensity, executor capabilities, and side effects are system-derived.
- A compound sentence may be split into an ordered event chain. Users may delete, rename, retarget, and reorder the resulting events before confirmation; no chat clarification loop is part of this release. Ambiguities are resolved into the most explainable plan and recorded in `assumptions` for internal audit, but those records do not become a standalone visible assumptions block.
- The time and intensity plan is generated from the event-mechanism graph. The ordinary surface shows a summary. Advanced correction is limited to step size/unit, total coverage or round count, event windows, derived intensity, and target regions/facilities; it never contains Effort, search mode, Agent count, Token limits, or relationship-search parameters.
- The user no longer chooses between baseline and crisis simulation modes. The frontend derives the legacy scenario mode from the submitted events and policies, and the backend may still persist a normalized `scenario_mode` for compatibility.
- Environment baseline is generated/display data. It is shown under the region display tab and must not be treated as a user-editable variable.
- Step 2 must not auto-submit on mount. Confirmation is the boundary that starts generation and locks the input form.
- Returning from Step 1 to an already visited Step 2 must reuse the saved Step 2 route when it points to an existing project or simulation. The Step 1 `进入场景配置` action should only create a new pending upload and enter `/process/new` when there is no reusable Step 2 route.
- Step 2 has exactly three user-facing states: `idle/error` is parameter configuration, `preparing` is generation in progress, and `ready` is result review. Do not render empty risk, region, Agent, or relationship panels before `ready`.
- The parameter section is the only primary content in `idle/error` and must be open by default. The confirm-and-generate action sits outside the form's scroll area so users never need to reach the bottom of a long variable list to start generation.
- In Step 1 configuration, the right panel shell, rounded border, title, and bottom action bar are fixed to the workspace height. Only the middle `.setup-form` scrolls; the outer `.setup-column` and `.setup-panel` must remain clipped and must never scroll their border or expose form content outside the card.
- Growing text fields in the Step 1 form must contribute their real textarea height to the scroll layout. A textarea may not overflow a collapsed label row and overlap the next field.
- Parameter DOM order must follow the task order used by sighted and keyboard users alike: scenario goal summary, disaster event chain, policy measures, automatic-plan summary, advanced corrections, and the fixed confirm action. Do not simulate this order with CSS `order`.
- The Step 2 automatic-plan summary is not a static instruction block. Before generation, it must dynamically summarize the current event chain, pending mechanism/time/intensity judgments, policy count, and RoleDemand output path. After a planning artifact is available, it may show actual generated counts such as mechanism nodes/edges, temporal coverage, policy plans, and role-demand classes.
- `preparing` replaces both the long form and result panels with real progress, the current stage, and this six-part sequence: parse compound events, build the mechanism/propagation graph, build temporal/spatial plans, extract role-capability demand, generate Agents/relationships through the current planning port, then assemble and validate the scene. The left graph may update progressively but must be labeled as a generation preview.
- In `ready`, risk objects, region division, Agent configuration, and relationship skeleton are peer tabs. Only the active result view is rendered; risk objects are the default. Locked parameters are available through a separate input snapshot and must not rejoin the result views as another long section.
- Ready-state risk selection is a compact horizontal rail above one full-width selected detail. The rail has no hard frontend item cap: it renders every risk returned by the contract, including larger future or historical collections. Previous/next buttons appear only when the rail actually overflows; a short or wide-enough rail has no permanent arrow controls, and narrow screens retain horizontal touch/trackpad scrolling instead of switching to a native select.
- Region review is comparison-first: one compact table shows identity, type, coverage, neighbor count, and no more than two representative attributes. The selected row opens a detail drawer for full attributes, risks, neighbors, and assigned Agents. Region cards must not expand into nested tag grids.
- Agent review is also comparison-first. Generated sets render a 12-row paginated table containing sequence, name, type, and primary region; only the current page exists in the DOM. A row may expand one bounded detail block, but the page must never mount every Agent as a card wall. If Agent V2 leaves `unresolved_demands`, the tab status must show partial coverage and list each unmet RoleDemand label with the Chinese audit reason. Do not imply the count is caused by Effort when the audit says missing spatial, institutional, or facility evidence.
- Relationship review explains the scenario interaction skeleton, not a raw edge dump. It must show readable source and target names, a localized relationship type, channel/strength when available, and a short rationale. Raw node fallback labels such as `未命名节点` or risk-object fallback names must never be counted as relationship types. Generic edges may be grouped into display categories such as information transfer, governance regulation, receptor exposure, facility dependency, risk impact, spatial association, or general association, but those categories are display summaries and must not mutate the backend relationship contract.
- Metadata chips use a thin outline and stay visually subordinate to names, counts, and status. Inline groups show at most two or three values plus `+N`; a full list belongs in the row detail or drawer.
- The ready-state action bar remains outside result scrolling as the workbench's flex tail sibling. It matches Step 1 with one top divider, no rounded outer container, no shadow, no four-side border, and no bottom gap. `进入推演` and the locked-input drawer entrance stay available while the active result tab scrolls.
- The Step 2 `.workspace-shell` remains the single result-scroll owner but is visually transparent: no full-height rounded background, border, or shadow. The rectangular right workbench is the only page surface; active result sections and actual data rows provide the internal hierarchy.
- System logs are internal diagnostics and are not part of the Step 2 business interface.
- Every Step 2 prepare/configuration response is projected at the simulation API boundary. Visible fields are localized while machine enums and identifiers remain available only for computation; exception details and traceback fields are removed regardless of which legacy route shape produced the response.
- New simulations use risk contract V2. A risk object is a read-only simulation anchor derived from a valid path of `trigger/source -> propagation mechanism -> real receptor -> concrete consequence`; it is not a generic keyword card and there is no minimum object count. A prepared scene may contain zero to eight active risk objects.
- Risk object copy must never expose internal variable fallback names such as `disaster_injection` or `policy_injection`. Step 2/3 should serialize a readable variable display name, and the backend risk builder must still sanitize historical/runtime payloads before composing `summary`, `why_now`, titles, or root pressures.
- V2 scoring has four explicit components: impact potential 35%, evidence sufficiency 30%, propagation potential 20%, and actionability 15%. `confidence_score` remains a compatibility projection equal to `evidence_strength_score / 100`; V2 UI must say `证据充分度`, never `置信度`.
- Every V2 object must preserve `risk_statement`, real mechanism node/edge IDs, scoped region/entity/Agent references, evidence and epistemic state, dedicated monitoring metrics, quality flags, stable source signature, and created round. Any unresolved reference disqualifies the candidate.
- Risk-object detail panels must separate real graph/map references from explanatory evidence. Evidence titles such as `...触发证据`, main risk families, colors, and internal IDs are never entity nodes. The visible structure is fixed as trigger, mechanism steps, receptor, consequence, affected regions/subjects, evidence state, and monitoring metrics. The causal chain must fit the selected detail's available width; lower groups use flat rows and dividers with at most two inline metadata values plus a remainder count, never blue nested containers or overflowing tag walls.
- In Chinese mode, Step 2 and the graph preview should avoid exposing raw internal labels when a stable Chinese label exists. Region tags such as `macro`, `coastal_zone`, `river_basin`, and `flood_risk` should render as Chinese.
- Region "agent coverage" is not the same as total agents. Total agents counts unique configured proxy bodies, while region coverage is a per-region aggregate across primary and influenced regions, so one agent may count in multiple region cards.
- The Agent panel should describe graph-derived records as temporary graph previews, not as a failure or degradation state.
- The Step 2 left map must render the same generated region, subregion, agent, and relationship projection used by the Step 2 result tabs. Demo fixtures such as the Wuhan replay and normal prepared simulations should share `SimulationView -> GraphPanel -> MapRelationPanel` and `/api/simulation/<id>/graph/realtime`.
- Fixes found through the Wuhan demo are product-flow fixes unless the issue is explicitly fixture-only.
- Visual tuning must preserve parity between fixture and formal flow. Line colors, line density rules, node halos, highlighted risk paths, relation filtering, and map/2D/3D mode behavior should live in shared Step 2/3 graph components or shared backend projection contracts. Do not patch only the Wuhan fixture to make it look right.
- The long-term parity rule remains that a generated graph/risk/agent contract change requires a Wuhan golden-artifact version bump. For the July 2026 Risk Object V2 main-flow rollout, the user explicitly deferred all Wuhan demo maintenance: the frozen fixture remains V1 and must not be regenerated or rewritten until a later unified demo-maintenance pass.
- Map relation layouts should avoid obvious repeated geometry templates. Deterministic jitter is acceptable and preferred for repeatable tests, but repeated same-direction triangles, spokes, and identical agent glyphs should be treated as fixture or projection defects.

## Step 2 Planning Contract

- The new `/api/simulation/prepare` request contains `simulation_id`, `event_inputs`, `policy_inputs`, `advanced_overrides`, and the locked `effort_snapshot_id`. New clients do not submit `search_mode`, `target_agent_count`, `search_profile_overrides`, or a manually selected `hazard_template_id`.
- `UserEventInput` preserves `input_id`, Chinese name/description, order, and region/entity targets. `UserPolicyInput` preserves `input_id`, Chinese name/intent, and region/entity targets. Historical `injected_variables` remain readable through a one-way compatibility projection and are not rewritten automatically.
- `ScenarioPlanner.build()` normalizes inputs, decomposes compound events, matches atomic mechanisms, creates causal/propagation edges, derives temporal phases and policy effects, extracts role-capability demand, and validates Chinese display fields and completeness.
- `EventMechanismGraph` is the scenario-specific source of truth. A legacy `hazard_template_id` may be projected by the compatibility layer but cannot control the whole scene or erase parallel propagation paths in a compound disaster.
- `TemporalPlan` derives step size, total rounds/coverage, event windows, propagation latency, persistence, intensity, and policy-effective windows. `PolicyPlan` describes intended effect primitives, target events/mechanisms/regions, required executor capabilities, trigger conditions, expected effects, side effects, and confidence; it does not bind a concrete Agent.
- Once `ScenarioPlanningInput` exists, its `TemporalPlan` is the runtime time source of truth. The legacy configuration generator must not call its old time planner or overwrite the reviewed step size, coverage, event windows, or policy windows. Runtime `time_plan`, `temporal_profile`, and `time_config` are compatibility projections of that same plan.
- Compound propagation remains multi-channel. The canonical mechanism graph may contain marine, atmospheric, river, and surface paths at the same time; legacy `hazard_template_id` and primary transport fields are compatibility projections only and must not erase or reprioritize those branches. New planned scenes mark this explicitly with `hazard_template_mode=compatibility_projection`.
- `RoleDemand` expresses required capabilities, jurisdiction, resolution, importance, and its causal event/mechanism references. It must not contain a concrete Agent ID or a target Agent count.
- The sole Agent planning boundary remains `ScenarioPlanningInput -> AgentPlanningPort`. New simulations use `AgentV2PlanningAdapter`, which delegates to `AgentPlannerV2`; `LegacyAgentPlanningAdapter` is retained only for historical and frozen-artifact compatibility.
- `AgentPlannerV2` resolves formal region references before placement, matches each `RoleDemand` to an authoritative `AgentArchetype`, merges compatible demands, and creates evidence-bounded `AgentInstance` records. A unique exact or fuzzy region match may resolve a reference; an unresolved reference is audited and never falls back to the first formal region. Legacy map/template profiles are candidate and evidence inputs only; they cannot override the V2 archetype's family, type, role, capability, permission, or resource boundary.
- The resulting simulation configuration persists the locked Effort snapshot and scenario-planning artifacts needed for review and runtime, including the event graph, temporal plan, policy plan, role demands, assumptions, `AgentPlan`, `PlacementPlan`, `ResolutionPlan`, and `PolicyExecutionPlan`. Unresolved role or target references remain explicit audit records instead of becoming guessed locations.
- Preparation is idempotent per simulation and planning hash. A repeated request with the same hash reuses the active task; a different hash is rejected while preparation is active so two workers never write the same simulation directory concurrently.
- When a client supplies an explicit preparation `task_id`, that task is authoritative for status polling. An older ready artifact for the same simulation must not make a newly forced preparation appear complete while its worker is still running.

## Release Scope

- Included here: Step 1 Effort selection/locking; Step 2 event and policy inputs, compound-event planning, automatic time/intensity planning, `PolicyPlan`, `RoleDemand`, Agent V2 planning, policy-executor binding, and the three-state review UI; Step 3 Agent action validation, dynamic relationships, policy execution, and runtime Agent activation/split/provisional creation.
- Explicitly excluded: the user-facing runtime variable-injection workflow, in-place Effort changes, chat clarification, complete mechanism-directed external re-query, full Step 4 counterfactual/sensitivity execution, and Wuhan frozen-fixture data migration.

## Risk Object V2 Generation

- `RiskDefinitionBuilder` is the public entry. V2 delegates candidate construction to `RiskCandidateExtractor`; the five legacy templates remain available only when `RISK_OBJECT_CONTRACT_VERSION=1` is explicitly selected.
- Inputs are injection variables, regions/subregions, entities, Agents, mechanism graph, validated relationship graph, scenario-state schema, and grounding summary. A minimal graph may be assembled only when the mechanism graph is absent. An explicit event graph with zero causal edges is incomplete and must produce zero risk objects; map adjacency or transport connectivity must never replace it.
- Ordinary candidates are simple directed paths with one to four mechanism edges. A feedback loop becomes `compound_cascade` only when it contains at least three unique nodes, crosses regions, has a real pressure source and terminal receptor, crosses at least two distinguishable system families, contains explicit feedback-amplification semantics, and independently passes the evidence gate. Spatial cycles and bidirectional transport links remain rejected audit candidates.
- A path made only of Agent-profile nodes and Agent-relationship edges is never a risk path, whether it is linear or cyclic. It is retained only as a rejected audit candidate because it has no pressure source, propagation process, or real receptor.
- An initial candidate needs at least one genuinely observed edge or two non-degraded inferred edges, plus evidence sufficiency of at least 40. `observed` requires a resolvable source record or a validated observed relationship; mentioning a real place in mechanism prose only resolves geographic scope and does not upgrade the edge's epistemic state. Any path containing a `speculative` edge remains audit-only and cannot enter the initial simulation.
- Dangling references, placeholder evidence, fallback/degraded relations, raw internal variable names, and color tokens are rejected before scoring. An unresolvable document/section citation is removed from user-facing evidence and recorded with `unresolved_source_citation_removed`; it is never presented as observed proof. Candidate IDs are SHA-256 signatures of source variable/source node, ordered mechanism edges, receptor, regions, and main family.
- Region scope is resolved only from real source-feature names, structured region aliases, explicit source-feature aggregation metadata, or cross-stage aliases that normalize Step 1 foundation IDs to formal runtime regions. Shared generic background prose must not make every risk reference every region. When exact entity-anchored regions exist, they take precedence over overlapping parent and automatically derived subregions. A candidate with no resolvable scene region is rejected.
- Paths with the same source and receptor merge only when mechanism-edge overlap is at least 60%. The highest-quality canonical path remains the primary path; duplicate variants are retained only as alternative paths and cannot inflate the primary mechanism chain beyond four edges. Candidates are sorted by the weighted score and the top eight become active; no synthetic card is created to satisfy a count target.
- Affected Agent references are scoped from the terminal mechanism/receptor to family-compatible `RoleDemand` records and then to profiles that also match the risk's region or entity scope. If role-demand contracts exist but no profile passes both checks, the risk keeps no Agent reference; validated relationship actors are not used as a broad fallback. A broad relation graph, same-family profile in another region, or policy executor must not make every risk show the same Agent list.
- Deterministic copy uses concrete receptor-system nouns and consequences, prefixes a generic receptor with its resolved region when necessary, and normalizes LLM titles into names ending in `风险`. Generic family names, `...触发证据`, color labels, and repeated placeholder titles are not valid substitutes for a receptor or consequence.
- The LLM receives only validated candidates. It runs at temperature 0.2 and may provide a Simplified-Chinese title, summary, consequence, open tags, and naming reason. It must return the rule-derived controlled family unchanged and cannot change references or add entity/region/evidence fields. Timeout, invalid JSON, missing IDs, changed family or mechanism references, or unauthorized fields cause a deterministic naming fallback.
- Controlled main families are `ecological_environment`, `health_safety`, `infrastructure_continuity`, `mobility_logistics`, `resource_supply`, `economy_livelihood`, `governance_response`, `information_trust`, `compound_cascade`, and `other_emergent`. Scenario-specific concepts belong in open Chinese tags.
- Mechanism nodes and edges remain visible in the relationship graph for causal inspection. A mechanism node is hidden from the geographic map unless it has a real geographic anchor; the projection must never invent a map position merely to display the path.

## Runtime Lifecycle

- Risk objects monitor and organize the simulation; they never feed Agent candidate discovery, Agent prompts, state deltas, transport coefficients, or mechanism propagation. Interventions still enter through policy variables, and risk objects compare their dedicated metrics before and after those interventions.
- Dedicated metrics are selected from the main family, receptor type, and scenario-state schema. Runtime aggregation is limited to each risk's referenced regions and path; it does not average every region in the scene.
- Runtime thresholds remain `watch < 52`, `elevated 52-71.9`, and `critical >= 72`. A risk resolves after three consecutive rounds below 35. The primary risk changes only when another active risk leads by at least five points.
- Runtime emergence executes after each round snapshot. A new signature must pass for two consecutive rounds, except when both evidence sufficiency and impact potential are at least 80. Successful promotion writes a `risk_emerged` event and `created_round`.
- No more than eight risks are active. Elevated and critical objects cannot be replaced. At capacity, a new risk must lead the lowest-priority watch object by at least ten points; the replaced object becomes dormant while its definition and events remain permanent.
- Variable injection never creates `risk_variable_N`. It changes variable and mechanism state; the shared emergence detector decides whether a new risk exists.

## Compatibility And Operations

- Artifacts without `risk_contract_version` are V1. Historical V1 runs and reports are read through the compatibility projection and are never rewritten automatically.
- New preparation defaults to V2. `RISK_OBJECT_CONTRACT_VERSION=1` is an emergency generation rollback only; it does not mutate existing V2 artifacts. Explicitly preparing a scene again creates a new V2 artifact set.
- V2 definitions are read-only. `/risk/pin` and `/risk/reframe` return `409 risk_definitions_read_only`; V1 retains its existing mutation behavior.
- The risk manifest and candidate ledger record candidate/active/rejected counts, dangling and invalid references, zero-object reason/rate, LLM participation/fallback, average active count, emergence count, and primary-risk switches.
- A zero-object V2 scene is valid. Step 2 shows `场景准备完成，未形成通过证据校验的风险对象`, and preparation, runtime, and reporting continue normally.
- On mobile, the shared Step 2/3 shell presents either the full-width workbench or the full-width graph. The existing graph toggle switches those local views; it must not compress the read-only risk details into an unusably narrow split pane.
- Risk Object V1/V2 compatibility remains independent from scenario-planning versioning. The Wuhan frozen planning fixture is refreshed to `scenario_planning.v2`; historical risk/runtime artifacts remain read-compatible and are not silently rewritten.
